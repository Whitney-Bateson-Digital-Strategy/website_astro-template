If food feels like a source of anxiety more than nourishment, you're not alone — and it doesn't have to stay that way.

## 1. You feel guilty after eating certain foods

Guilt is a signal that a food has been assigned moral weight it doesn't actually have. Food isn't a measure of your character. When eating something triggers a wave of shame, that's worth paying attention to.

## 2. You think about food constantly

Some food thoughts are normal — you get hungry, you think about what sounds good. But if thoughts about what you ate, what you'll eat, or whether you "should" eat something are taking up significant mental bandwidth, that's a sign of restriction or a strained relationship.

## 3. You have a long list of "bad" foods

The longer your list of forbidden foods, the more fraught eating becomes. Foods don't have moral values. Some are more nutrient-dense than others, but no single food will make or break your health.

## 4. You eat differently in public than in private

This often points to shame around eating — performing "healthy" for others while feeling free to eat normally only when alone. Neither version is wrong. But the gap between them often causes stress.

## 5. Eating "off plan" derails your whole day

One unplanned cookie shouldn't send the day into a spiral. When it does, it usually signals that the "plan" is too rigid to survive contact with real life.

---

If any of these feel familiar, the good news is that these patterns can shift with support. That's exactly what working with a dietitian can help with — not just what to eat, but how to feel about eating.
